Common Questions

Frequently asked questions.

Everything you need to know about our programs, participation, volunteering, and partnership opportunities.

Children are identified through community referrals, church partnerships, and home visits by our social workers. Eligibility is based on vulnerability factors including poverty level, family structure, and access to basic needs. Applications are reviewed by our enrollment committee, with priority given to children experiencing extreme poverty, those from single-parent households, and orphans. The enrollment process includes home assessments, caregiver interviews, and orientation sessions.
Holistic development means we nurture every dimension of a child's growth — spiritual, physical, socio-emotional, and economic. This includes weekly biblical formation and worship, health screenings and nutrition support, life skills training and mentorship, academic tutoring and career guidance, as well as vocational skills like agriculture, tailoring, and entrepreneurship. We believe poverty is more than material lack; it's the absence of opportunity, relationship, and hope. Our programs address all these dimensions.
There are many ways to partner with us! You can volunteer as a tutor, mentor, or event helper (minimum commitment of 4 hours/month). Financial partnerships can sponsor a child (KES 3,500/month), support specific programs like our agricultural plots or holiday intensives, or contribute to capital projects like our upcoming multipurpose hall. Prayer partnership is equally valued — join our monthly prayer gatherings or receive our quarterly prayer guide. International partners can engage through Compassion International's sponsorship network. Contact us to explore the best fit for your gifts and availability.
The Child Survival Program (CSP) supports pregnant mothers and caregivers of children from birth to age 3. It's our earliest intervention point, recognizing that the first 1,000 days of life are critical for development. Mothers receive prenatal care support, nutrition education, parenting workshops, and spiritual formation. Babies receive regular health checkups, immunizations, and developmental assessments. Currently, 40 infants are enrolled, with their mothers participating in weekly group sessions and monthly home visits from our trained caregivers.
Participants typically complete the program around age 18-22, depending on their educational path. Graduation isn't an ending but a transition. Graduates join our alumni network, receiving continued mentorship, career guidance, and access to micro-loan programs for entrepreneurial ventures. Many return as volunteers, tutors, or even staff members. Our goal is to see every graduate become a self-reliant, Christ-centered adult who contributes positively to their community. We track graduate outcomes for five years post-program to ensure lasting impact.
KE0274 PEFA Mfangano CYDC operates as a partnership between PEFA Cathedral Sena and Compassion International Kenya. Primary funding comes through Compassion's child sponsorship model, supplemented by individual donors, church partnerships, and occasional grants for specific projects. Governance includes a local board comprising church leadership, community representatives, parent representatives, and Compassion field staff. We undergo annual audits, quarterly reviews, and regular program evaluations to ensure accountability and excellence in stewardship.
